Plant Disease Detection Using Deep Learning & CNN

A comprehensive final-year engineering system utilizing MobileNetV2 transfer learning, computer vision preprocessing, and a full-stack Flask agricultural dashboard.

Problem Statement

Plant diseases threaten global food security, accounting for 20% to 40% of agricultural yield loss annually. Traditional diagnosis requires trained plant pathologists conducting visual inspection or time-consuming lab microbiological assays.

For smallholder farmers and agricultural extension agents, delays in pathogen identification lead to improper pesticide usage, increased input costs, and irreversible crop failure. An automated, accessible, and accurate visual diagnosis system is urgently required.

Project Objectives

  • Automated Leaf Classification: Train a CNN to detect healthy vs diseased leaves across 38 categories from the PlantVillage dataset.
  • Transfer Learning with MobileNetV2: Leverage feature representations pretrained on ImageNet for high classification accuracy with low compute overhead.
  • Actionable Guidance: Map model outputs to structured agricultural disease descriptions, symptom checklists, and integrated management tips.
  • Full-Stack Web Interface: Deploy a user-friendly Flask application with real-time image upload, validation, and inference feedback.

Deep Learning Methodology: MobileNetV2 Transfer Learning

MobileNetV2 was chosen over heavier networks (like VGG16 or ResNet50) because it achieves state-of-the-art accuracy while operating with significantly fewer parameters (3.5M vs 138M in VGG16) and low computational latency.

Input Layer
224 × 224 × 3 RGB

Normalized to [-1, 1] range

Feature Extractor
MobileNetV2 Base

Pretrained on ImageNet-1K

Regularization
GAP & Dropout

Global Avg Pooling + 0.3 / 0.2 Dropout

Classification Head
Dense Softmax

38 Output Probability Nodes

Key MobileNetV2 Architectural Innovations:
Depthwise Separable Convolutions: Factorizes a standard convolution into a depthwise convolution (spatial filtering per channel) followed by a 1×1 pointwise convolution (channel combination), reducing computational costs by 8 to 9 times.
Inverted Residuals & Linear Bottlenecks: Shortcuts link low-dimensional bottleneck layers rather than expanded manifolds, preserving gradient flow while linear activations prevent non-linearities from destroying manifold manifolds.

PlantVillage Dataset

The PlantVillage benchmark dataset contains over 54,000 expertly classified foliar images across 14 crop species and 26 diseases alongside healthy controls.

Property Specification
Total Images ~54,305 leaf photos
Total Classes 38 distinct plant/condition categories
Crop Species Tomato, Potato, Corn, Apple, Grape, Pepper, Peach, etc.
Split Strategy 70% Train, 15% Validation, 15% Test (Stratified)
Data Augmentation Random Rotation (±15°), Flip, Zoom (±15%), Shift

System Limitations & Future Scope

Current Limitations:
  • Lab vs Field Discrepancy: PlantVillage images were captured on uniform neutral backgrounds. Complex field clutter may reduce accuracy.
  • Single-Leaf Focus: The system evaluates individual leaves and does not currently detect whole-canopy or multi-disease co-infections.
  • Out-of-Distribution Inputs: Leaves from unmodeled plant species or non-leaf photos will receive predictions from the closest modeled class unless low confidence is detected.
Future Scope:
  • Edge Deployment: Convert the `.keras` model to TensorFlow Lite (`.tflite`) for offline deployment on Android devices using Flutter.
  • Multi-Spectral & Drone Integration: Integrate drone multispectral camera feeds for macro-field stress mapping.
  • Explainable AI (XAI): Implement Grad-CAM heatmap visualizations to highlight exact lesion areas driving neural predictions.
